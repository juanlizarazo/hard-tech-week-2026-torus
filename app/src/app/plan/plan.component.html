Three lanes share one time axis, 15 minutes per column, across the planning day and the day after.

  • Bill: dark blocks are the tariff's on-peak hours, labeled with the energy price. On Schedule 8 only these hours set the demand charge. On Schedule 6 every hour can, so the lane is hatched.
  • Grid price: PACE's day-ahead wholesale price from EDAM. Light is cheap, dark blue is expensive. Hatched cells are hours not yet posted.
  • Carbon: marginal CO2, the extra CO2 from one more MWh. Light is cleaner, dark brown is dirtier.
  • Orange stripes: rush hour, the busiest four hours of PACE's own forecast. The second day's window is assumed to repeat.

Good looks like the plan charging in light cells and discharging in dark ones, while keeping the grid draw low in the stripes.
  • Black line: what the site would pull from Rocky Mountain Power with no storage, in kW.
  • Green area: the planned grid draw with the Torus Station working. Where green is below black, the station is feeding the site.
  • Dashed line: the billed demand the plan holds. One 15-minute interval above it would raise the whole month's demand charge.
  • Orange stripes: rush hour.

Good looks like a flat green top at the dashed line and a dip inside the stripes. Green above black outside rush hour is the station recharging.
  • Top panel: power from each machine in kW. Above zero feeds the site; below zero charges from the grid.
  • Blue: Nova Spin, the flywheel. High power for minutes, about 25,000 cycles of life.
  • Purple: Nova Pulse, the battery. Hours of energy, but ramp-limited and it wears about three times faster per kWh.
  • Bottom panel: state of charge of each machine as a percent of its usable range. The dashed line is the reserve floor when a Wattsmart reserve is set.
  • Orange stripes: rush hour.

Good looks like blue bursts at the sharp edges and purple carrying the long blocks, so the battery is not worn out by spikes.
